RIDEDECK / BLUETOOTH INVESTIGATION

Tracing how my motorcycle talks to my phone.

Building RideDeck led me into investigating the communication between Yamaha Y-Connect and my XMAX 2024. I wanted to understand the data behind the app and what could be used in my own motorcycle companion.

FROM OBSERVATION TO IMPLEMENTATION

Following the evidence in the Bluetooth traffic

Android diagnosticsBluetooth HCI / BLEPythonProtocol analysis

Capture what actually happens

I recorded Android Bluetooth HCI traffic while the original Y-Connect app communicated with my bike, then used nRF Connect to inspect the Bluetooth services and characteristics. With the bike parked and the engine off, I tested the front and rear brakes, indicators and hazards individually, recording known on/off periods for comparison.

Turn packets into something understandable

The investigation included Python tools to validate capture records, reassemble fragmented messages and decode protocol envelopes and protobuf data. I compared the captures with stored app and SDK metadata to understand message structure, authentication and the distinction between the navigation and vehicle-data connections.

Check the interpretation

I compared candidate fields with readings in Y-Connect and checked whether changes followed the controlled tests. A changing byte is only a clue: it can represent a counter, timing information or another sensor. Decoder checks covered missing fragments, malformed lengths and traffic direction.

What this established

The work identified observed services, message structures, supported data identifiers and authentication exchanges from the original app. Further source and capture analysis traced model-specific parsing and fuel calculations. I checked candidate values against recorded engine activity and rejected interpretations that the evidence did not support. Reliable physical units, brake and indicator meanings, and fuel estimates still require validation.

This project brings together practical troubleshooting, software development and testing against real hardware. RideDeck is an independent project with no Yamaha affiliation. Raw captures and authentication details remain private.
