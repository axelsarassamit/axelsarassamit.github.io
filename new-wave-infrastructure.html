NEW WAVE GROUP / ORREFORS KOSTA BODA / JANUARY 2023 TO FEBRUARY 2025

Infrastructure and site IT at New Wave

My work connected central IT with the equipment, systems and people at the sites. I worked with Mac management, migrations, servers, networks, backups and the IT used in manufacturing, shops, hotels, offices and the new arena.

I was employed by Orrefors Kosta Boda AB and worked in the wider New Wave central IT environment. The group had more than 5,000 users, with central IT colleagues in Sweden, Belgium and the Netherlands and local IT contacts at the individual companies.

That organization shaped how I worked. I could be dealing with a server-room problem on site, a migration with the PM, a Mac user facing a catalogue deadline or a local contact at another company. The technical work and the coordination could not be separated.

01 / BUILDING MACOPS

I built the Jamf environment and the team around it.

I built the group’s Jamf Pro environment from the start, integrated Apple Business Manager and set up zero-touch enrolment. I owned and coordinated the Apple-management workstream and formed the team delivering and supporting MacOps.

The MacOps team had two to three people and supported approximately 500 Mac users/devices across the group. That wider fleet included creative users who depended on their computers to produce work to fixed deadlines.

Understanding what the user needed to do

Getting a computer enrolled and making the environment work for the person using it were different parts of the job. Creative teams had catalogue dates and other deadlines. A print problem, an application issue or a change to where someone’s work was stored could become urgent very quickly.

I needed to understand that pressure while working through the technical problem. Supporting MacOps meant building the management environment and the relationships needed to keep people working.

I also needed the work to continue through a team. The environment could not depend on me personally setting up or fixing every computer. Team formation, coordination and documentation were part of the responsibility.

02 / MIGRATIONS AND PROJECT TEAMS

Different projects had different teams.

I led the 12-person project team for the move from on-premise Active Directory to Microsoft 365 and Entra ID, including OneDrive. I worked with the PM on the pre-study, the approach, the priorities and the execution.

The file migration was one of the most challenging parts of my work. The amount of data and the way people used it were not obvious from the original server list. Finance reporting dates, catalogue deadlines and the condition of the old servers all affected the sequence.

I also led the separate 10-person KACE-to-Intune migration team as an MDM expert. That project concerned the Windows deployment and management environment, rather than being the same work as the file and identity migration.

Other workstreams

My responsibilities also included a two-person backup team, a three-person print team and a four-person Exchange mail team. I worked with PMO or team coordinators across the projects. These were different groups with different jobs, rather than one permanent team added together.

I had to coordinate work across those responsibilities and keep the people involved informed. That included explaining when a proposed schedule did not fit a business deadline or when a technical problem meant the plan needed to change.

03 / SERVERS, NETWORKS AND WORKPLACE SERVICES

I worked with the physical equipment and the services running on it.

My infrastructure work included VMware vSphere/ESXi, Windows Server, Active Directory and Microsoft Azure, alongside Microsoft 365 administration and endpoint management. At Kosta I also worked hands-on with the server-room equipment.

The physical environment included Dell PowerEdge servers, NAS/SAN storage, racks, cable connections and UPS equipment. Deploying or changing equipment meant dealing with the actual installation as well as the service it supported.

The Windows and directory work included users, Group Policy, DNS and DHCP. In Microsoft 365 I worked with the environment around Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive and Entra ID, alongside Intune. These services were part of the wider group environment, with approximately 3,500 client desktops.

Networking and security

I worked with Aruba and Meraki networking and Palo Alto systems, including GlobalProtect VPN and Cortex XDR in the wider security environment. The work sat alongside Windows services, device support and the site networks people used every day.

Cabling was a large part of the practical work. I tested and replaced cables and used Fluke and SignalTEK tools in my network work. I wanted clear records of the connections, not a rack that needed to be understood again every time someone opened it.

While working on site I recorded cable names, switches and ports in Excel. Afterwards I entered or imported the records into NetBox, followed the connections and went back to check anything that looked wrong.

Incident and change records

I used SysAid for incident handling, change records and SLA tracking. The record needed to connect what someone reported with the work carried out and the people involved. It was part of managing ongoing operations across different sites and teams.

04 / BACKUPS AND CRITICAL INCIDENTS

The recovery work had to be practical.

I led the two-person backup team. My work included Arcserve backup administration, Dell tape equipment, restore drills and checking recovery objectives. I also worked with changes to the backup equipment and the ongoing tasks around it.

A backup job was connected to the equipment, the data and the ability to recover. The restore work mattered because the team needed to understand the recovery process, rather than only know where the backup system was.

The cooling incident

We had an air-conditioning failure in a server-room environment. It required a quick response. Afterwards, we revised the emergency cooling procedures and added monitoring in the room.

Afterwards, we needed the next response to be understood and prepared for. It should not depend on the same people being present and remembering what they did last time.

I was part of New Wave’s emergency team for critical IT incidents and infrastructure response. That work brought together the physical installation, the monitoring, the documentation and the people who could act on the problem.

05 / HOSPITALITY AND VENUE IT

The hotel reopening and the arena were different projects.

Kosta Boda Art Hotel

I supported the IT for the reopening of Kosta Boda Art Hotel. The work covered the network and systems around the hotel, including Hotsoft PMS, POS and payment terminals, room connectivity, access and key-card systems, printing and conference AV.

The hospitality environment also included restaurant and kitchen-display systems, spa integrations and the connections between guest-facing and staff systems. Guest Wi-Fi and the staff network had different purposes and were separated in the network design.

That meant the site work extended beyond office computers. Reception, restaurant, spa, conference and room systems depended on the infrastructure, while each area had its own daily work and its own people to coordinate with.

The new CRAFT Arena

CRAFT Arena was a new venue build. My work included network and Wi-Fi infrastructure and the POS used for visitor-facing operations. It was separate from the hotel reopening, with its own installation and operating context.

I worked on the IT supporting the venue. The same practical concerns applied as elsewhere: the connections, the equipment and the people using it had to fit together. The site needed usable infrastructure, not only a list of installed devices.

Shops and visitor areas

I also supported retail and visitor-facing operations around Kosta, including the outlet, factory shop and related areas. POS terminals, card readers, receipt printers, barcode scanners and the network behind them were part of the work.

06 / MANUFACTURING AND LOGISTICS

Supporting the systems behind the work

My site responsibilities included the glass-manufacturing environment, offices and warehouse/logistics infrastructure. Those users depended on connectivity, devices and printing in ways that were different from a desk-based office user.

The warehouse work included network connectivity and support for barcode and label devices. Zebra mobile computers and scanners, label printers and the Windows/server and print services around them were part of that environment.

I worked on the infrastructure layer and coordinated with application owners, vendors and local IT contacts when a business application was involved. The people responsible for the warehouse application still owned that application; my work supported the systems and devices it depended on.

Printing was its own responsibility.

I also led the print workstream, covering cloud and centralized print management. Xerox and HP equipment was used across office, retail and hospitality areas. The work connected device configuration, the print environment and the person trying to produce the document or label.

This is one reason I do not describe the role as only Mac management or only server administration. The infrastructure work ran into the business at many different points, and I had to understand what the user was trying to do.

07 / PEOPLE, PURCHASING AND HANDOVER

I could not rely on direct authority over every local contact.

The central IT organization was in Sweden, Belgium and the Netherlands, while the individual companies and sites had their own IT contacts. I worked across that structure and travelled to Belgium and the Netherlands for deployment and standardization work.

Getting a change delivered needed patience, negotiation and an understanding of the local situation. I had to work with people who had their own responsibilities and priorities, rather than simply tell everyone what to do.

PMO and the technical work

I worked closely with PMO and team coordinators. One lesson from the migration was that technical people need to be involved early. The PM and management do not automatically know how much data is involved, what is happening on the old equipment or which business deadlines affect the work.

I helped fill those gaps and turn them into something the team could execute. I also documented the method so colleagues could continue without every task depending on me.

Purchasing and suppliers

I brought purchasing and supplier experience from my earlier work. At New Wave I found better prices and purchasing channels for IT parts, cables and materials. I needed the right equipment for the job and a supplier commitment the team could plan around.

My approach was to communicate clearly, ask for written commitments and look for a practical solution when something was delayed. The same approach applied whether I was dealing with a local site, another team or a supplier.
