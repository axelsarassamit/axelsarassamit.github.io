NEW WAVE GROUP / 2023 TO 2025 ROLE

Microsoft 365 migration across a decentralized group.

Leading the AD, Microsoft 365, Entra ID and OneDrive migration workstream meant coordinating a project team, PMO and local IT contacts across independently operated companies.

My responsibilityI led a 12-person project team for the migration from on-premise Active Directory to Microsoft 365 and Entra ID, including OneDrive. This was a project-specific team, not a claim of 12 permanent direct reports.

A defined workstream within a wider environment.

My work at New Wave Group included central infrastructure, endpoint operations and the Apple management workstream. The Microsoft 365 migration was a distinct project responsibility within that wider role.

02 / DELIVERY

Progress depended on the people at each company.

The group operated through a decentralized structure. Central IT was based in Sweden, Belgium and the Netherlands, while individual companies and sites had their own local IT contacts. Delivering a shared migration required patience, negotiation and coordination with people outside my direct reporting line.

Work with PMO and team coordinators

I worked with PMO or team coordinators across the projects. My responsibility was to connect technical work with delivery coordination and keep the relevant teams moving toward the project outcome.

Connect central and local responsibilities

The central team provided the infrastructure and shared direction. Local contacts brought the context of their own company and site. My role required working across that boundary, rather than relying on direct authority over every person involved.

This is the part of migration leadership I want to make visible: understanding the technical dependencies while getting work delivered through a distributed organization.

03 / TECHNICAL CONTEXT

Identity, endpoints and user services are connected.

The documented migration scope connects on-premise directory identity, Microsoft cloud identity and OneDrive. Those layers affect how people sign in and reach their working environment. My wider responsibilities also covered Microsoft 365 administration and endpoint management.

That context matters, but separate projects remain separate. I also led a 10-person KACE-to-Intune migration team as an MDM expert and a four-person Exchange mail team. Their team sizes are not added to the Microsoft 365 team, and their work is not presented as one combined migration.

DELIVERY IN PRACTICE

The migration plan had to reflect how people actually worked.

In my interview account, I described this as one of my most challenging and rewarding projects. The work was larger than the initial view of the servers suggested: departments held different amounts of data, people used different storage arrangements, and moving the files was only part of the problem.

Pre-study with the project manager

I worked with the PM to fill gaps in the technical picture before execution: what data was involved, which tools and approach to use, where to start, and which users and business roles needed particular attention. Existing local storage and cloud copies also needed to be understood.

Plan around business deadlines

Finance had reporting dates. Creative teams had catalogue deadlines. I raised those dependencies in planning meetings and worked with the PM to adjust the sequence, rather than treating every department as interchangeable.

Change priority when the risk changes

Server support dates and disk health affected the order of work. In one example I described, a storage failure meant a company scheduled for a later migration needed urgent attention. The plan had to change to reflect the infrastructure risk.

Explain the working restrictions

During the relevant file-transfer stage, users needed to understand when they could not change the source data. That required patient communication with people who still had their own work to deliver.

Make the process usable by the whole team

I documented the method so colleagues could carry out the work without every migration depending on me. The handover also covered what happened to the on-premise servers as they reached retirement. My lesson was to involve technical staff earlier, make business dependencies visible, and build a process others could continue.

These examples are paraphrased from my first-hand interview accounts. They describe my decisions and responsibilities; they are not independently measured performance results.
