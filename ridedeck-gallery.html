RideDeck screenshots

Screenshots from my installed apps in Light and Dark, portrait and landscape. Road scenes, telemetry, calls, email and maps use sample data.

What I am showing here

I develop RideDeck as three separate Android apps: the main phone companion, the Yamaha edition and Dash. They share a design, but they have different jobs and separate source and signed update builds.

These are screenshots from the installed apps. I use them to check how the controls, settings and information fit together in portrait and landscape and in Light and Dark modes. The road scenes, telemetry, calls, email and map examples use sample data so I can show the interface without publishing private messages or recordings.

The screens are part of the development and phone testing. Motorcycle-display hardware, real-call audio, GPS accuracy and long or screen-off recording need their own checks.

RideDeck

The main app brings phone controls, music, readable messages and external navigation into one place. It opens the selected map app rather than treating every navigation service as the same built-in system.

I want the routine controls to be easy to find. The main-screen captures show the music area and message box, while the appearance and Setup screens show how the common choices are organized.

The Light, Dark, portrait and landscape views are different checks of the same interface. A layout that looks right in one orientation still needs to keep the controls and message area readable when the phone is turned.

RideDeck for Yamaha

The Yamaha edition adds its own navigation tools and a separate motorcycle-map display. I grouped the Yamaha controls together and flattened the Bike display and maps settings so the related choices can be followed on one page.

The gallery includes the app-rendered motorcycle-display preview with a sample route. It also shows what happens to a long turn instruction inside the fixed guidance area. That lets me check the text behaviour without presenting the preview as a test of a connected motorcycle display.

The call and email examples show the interface using sample content. Email is read-only and includes Read aloud. The call-presentation work is implemented in the interface, while real-call audio remains a separate test.

This work also connects to my investigation of how Y-Connect communicates with my motorcycle. I captured Bluetooth traffic, inspected services and compared controlled tests. The investigation is described separately below the portfolio’s RideDeck section.

RideDeck Dash

Dash is the recording companion. The interface brings together a framed camera view, Record, Stop and Protect controls, saved clips and a video editor. The editor includes trimming, framing, audio and telemetry choices.

The road pictures and clips in these examples are synthetic. They let me check the interface and editor without publishing someone’s real ride. The saved-clip screens include a clearly named demo clip.

Dash can follow the host app’s appearance through authenticated synchronization or keep its own override. I checked the themes and the editor in both orientations because the recording screen and editing controls have different space requirements.

Background recording depends on the authenticated companion ride session and remains subject to the phone’s power limits. A working screen or a loaded editor is not the same thing as a long recording test, so I keep those checks separate.
